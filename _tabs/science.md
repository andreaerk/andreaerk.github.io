## Stars and Supernovae
Textbook stellar evolution looks clean and simple. "We understand stars" is what you would assume. Reality is much different. Stars are messy and complicated physical systems, where nuclear physics, fluid dynamics, gravity, radiation transport, and even condense-matter physics come together in one place together.  Moreover, stars live in binaries, they rotate, some have magnetic fields, different metallicities, different masses...  

In simple terms.... it's hard. And from this complicated mess, the most interesting astrophysical phenomena emerge: from supernovae, to black holes and neutron stars, to gravitational wave mergers. Supernovae are especially interesting, as they also represent a incredible intersection of extreme physics. Each supernova carries the fingerprints of its progenitor star, so studying supernovae ultimately tells us something about stars. 

My main research focuses on studying stars, their weird evolution in binaries, and their supernova explosions. On the side, I enjoy also a bit of exoplanets.
