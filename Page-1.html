Interacting supernovae from wide massive binary systems

Andrea Ercolino, Harim Jin, Norbert Langer & Luc Dessart
  Astronomy & Astrophysics Volume 685, id.A58, May 2024   Astronomy & Astrophysics 
Volume 685, id.A58, May 2024

Binary star evolution shapes the lives and deaths of massive stars through mass-transfer between the two stars. What happens when mass-transfer occurs in the last thousands of years before the supernova explosion? In this theoretical study,​ we showed that wide binaries, in which a Red Supergiant undergoes this "late" mass-transfer with its companion, can naturally produce a remarkable diversity of circumstellar material (CSM) around the supernova progenitor. The presence of massive CSM will likely turn the explosion into an "Interacting supernova", like so-called Type IIn or SLSN-II supernovae, which are known to have diverse CSM properties.  Rather than requiring exotic pre-supernova eruptions, our work demonstrates that binary evolution alone provides a unified framework for understanding much of the remarkable diversity of interacting supernovae. Furthermore, the presence of extended atmospheres on top of the Red Supergiant can also explain the high-density environments inferred from "flash-ionization" supernovae, again without requiring significant pre-explosion mass-loss. 

Ercolino et al. 2024

Mass-transferring binary stars as progenitors of interacting hydrogen-free supernovae

Andrea Ercolino, Harim Jin, Norbert Langer & Luc Dessart
  Astronomy & Astrophysics ​Volume 696, id.A103, April 2025   Astronomy & Astrophysics 
Volume 696, id.A103, April 2025
Ercolino et al. 2025

Most hydrogen-poor supernovae are thought to originate from stars that lost their hydrogen envelopes to a binary companion long before they explode. However, mass-transfer may also manifest close to the time when the star dies. In this follow-up theoretical study, we showed that mass-transferring binary stars can produce dense, hydrogen-free circumstellar material (CSM) around the binary just before core collapse. When the supernova ejecta collide with this helium-rich CSM, the explosion can appear as a rare class of interacting hydrogen-free supernovae, offering a natural explanation for events such as Type Ibn supernovae and other rapidly evolving interacting transients. The CSM may potentially be found in a disk, rather than being spherically-symmetrically distributed, as typically assumed when inferring properties from observed transients.

The demographics of core-collapse supernovae
The role of binary evolution and interaction with the circumstellar medium

Andrea Ercolino, Harim Jin, Norbert Langer, Avishay Gal-Yam, Abel Schootemeijer, Caroline Mannes
  Astronomy & Astrophysics ​​Volume 706, id.A169, February 2026

What determines the different kinds of supernovae we observe across the Universe? In this theoretical study, we combined detailed stellar evolution models with the observed diversity of core-collapse supernovae to investigate how binary interactions shape their final explosions. We showed that binary evolution naturally broadens the range of supernova progenitors, producing the observed mix of hydrogen-rich, stripped-envelope, and interacting supernovae. In particular, late-time mass transfer can account for the observed rates and properties of interacting supernovae, while binary stripping explains the lower ejecta masses of many stripped-envelope explosions. Our work demonstrates that binary evolution is a key ingredient for understanding the demographics of core-collapse supernovae, providing a unified evolutionary framework that connects the lives of massive stars to the rich diversity of explosions we observe.

Ercolino et al. 2026 a
